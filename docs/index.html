What it does

Made for one thing: never missing a prayer.

The next prayer, always in view

A single inverted card carries the next salah and a monospaced countdown, so one glance tells you exactly what is left.

Five times, at a glance

Fajr through Isha, each with its Arabic name, its time in 12 or 24 hour format, and the next one marked.

Reminders on your terms

At the adhan, or 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes after. Sound and vibration are separate switches, and you can test one before you trust it.

Works without a connection

The day's schedule is cached on the device. Lose signal and the same times stay on screen, marked as offline.

Two themes, one palette

Pure Light and Deep Dark, both built from black, white and a single hairline. No colour competing with the content.

No account, no ads, no tracking

There is no sign-in, no analytics SDK, no advertising ID and no location permission. Nothing about you leaves the phone.

Quick answers

About the apps

Clear details on availability, privacy, and what each app does.

Are these Android apps free?

Yes. Namaaz Reminder, Screen Time Reminder, and Subscription Tracker are free to download from their GitHub releases. They have no ads.

What does Subscription Tracker do?

It lets you manually record subscriptions and recurring bills, see normalized monthly and yearly spending, track upcoming renewal dates, and receive optional reminders before payments are due.

Does Subscription Tracker connect to my bank?

No. You add costs and renewal dates yourself. The app does not connect to a bank, and subscription data stays stored locally on your phone.

Which app gives prayer times and adhan reminders?

Namaaz Reminder shows daily prayer times for cities in India, a countdown to the next prayer, and optional reminders at adhan.

Do the apps require an account or track me?

The apps are designed without accounts, advertising, or analytics tracking. Subscription Tracker works offline and keeps its data on-device.
