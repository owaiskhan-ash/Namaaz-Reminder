Just Apps / Apps / Namaaz Reminder

01 · Prayer

Prayer times for your city, and a countdown to the next salah.

Namaaz Reminder shows today’s Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha times for your city in India, counts down to the next prayer, and can remind you at adhan. It is free, has no ads, and asks for no account.

In one line: Namaaz Reminder is a free Android prayer-times app for India that shows daily salah times, a live countdown and optional adhan reminders — without ads, accounts or a location permission.

Coming to Google Play Privacy policy

Version 1.3.1 for Android 7.0 and later. There is no public download while the app is being prepared for the Play Store.

What it does

  • Today’s five prayers — Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha for the city you pick, in 12- or 24-hour format.
  • A live countdown to the next salah, so you can see at a glance how long is left.
  • Reminders at the adhan, or a set number of minutes after it, for any or all prayers.
  • A city picker that works by name — Delhi by default, or any Indian city you type.
  • A choice of calculation method, with the method used across India selected by default.
  • An offline copy of the last schedule that was fetched, so the screen still opens without a connection.

How the prayer times are calculated

Timings are not bundled with the app. When you open it, Namaaz Reminder asks the Aladhan prayer-times API for today’s schedule, sending only three things: the city name, the country (India) and the number of the calculation method you chose. The API returns the five timings and the city’s time zone, and the app draws the countdown from the device clock.

Different schools and organisations use slightly different solar angles for Fajr and Isha, which is why the method is a setting rather than a fixed decision. The default is method 1, the University of Islamic Sciences, Karachi convention that is common across India. If your mosque follows a different convention, pick the matching method in Settings and the times will move to match it. Individual prayers can also be corrected by hand for a given day; long-pressing a corrected time restores the calculated one.

Because the last successful response is kept on the phone, the schedule is re-anchored to the current date when the app is offline. If the cached copy is several days old, a timing can be off by a minute or two — the screen says so rather than pretending the data is live.

Reminders that behave

Prayer reminders are scheduled on the device with Android’s alarm manager, not sent from a server. That has three consequences worth knowing: they work with no connection, they survive a reboot (the app re-arms them at startup), and no notification content ever leaves your phone.

By default a reminder fires five minutes after the prayer time — the point at which the adhan has finished and the salah begins — and the offset is adjustable from zero upwards. Sound and vibration are separate switches, individual prayers can be muted, and the Madinah adhan can be replaced with an audio file of your own.

What stays on your phone

There is no account and no sync. Everything the app knows lives in its own private storage on the device.

Location permission
Not requested. The app never reads GPS, Wi-Fi or cell-tower position.
Sent over the network
The city name, the country and the calculation method, to api.aladhan.com over HTTPS. Nothing else is attached.
Stored on the device
Your settings and today’s schedule, in the app’s private storage. Removed when you uninstall.
Analytics and ads
None. No advertising SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, no tracking.

Common questions

Does Namaaz Reminder need an internet connection?

Only to fetch the timings. Once a schedule has been downloaded it is cached on the phone, so the main screen and the countdown keep working offline. Reminders are scheduled on the device and need no connection at all.

Does it use my GPS location?

No. The app does not request Android’s location permission and never reads GPS, Wi-Fi or cell-tower position. You choose a city by name, and that name is the only location information involved.

When do the reminders fire?

By default five minutes after the prayer time, which is when the adhan has ended and the salah begins. The offset is adjustable in Settings down to zero, and individual prayers can be muted.

Which calculation method is used?

Method 1 — the University of Islamic Sciences, Karachi convention — is the default because it is the most common choice in India. Other methods can be selected in Settings, and any single prayer time can be corrected by hand for the day.

Is it free?

Yes. Namaaz Reminder is free, has no ads, no subscriptions and no in-app purchases, and it does not ask you to create an account.

Which cities are supported?

Any city you can name, with Delhi as the default. The request is made against India as the country and the API returns the city’s own time zone.
